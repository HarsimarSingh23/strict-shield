How Strict Shield filters a page

  1. Network rules (before anything downloads). Chrome checks every request against the extension's block lists (declarativeNetRequest). Requests to ad and tracker domains are cancelled inside the browser, so the ad never loads. The lists are in filters/ads.txt and filters/trackers.txt in the source.
  2. Popup guard (in the page). Pages open popups with window.open(), links with target="_blank", hidden link clicks and form submits. A script that runs before the page's own scripts wraps those calls and cancels any that would open a different site. Same-site popups still work.
  3. Popup backstop (in the browser). If a new tab still appears, the extension compares its address with the site of the tab that opened it. A different site means the tab is closed before it can load.
  4. Navigation guard. When a page tries to send its own tab to another site (a redirect, a script, an ad frame, a link), the navigation is stopped and you stay on the page, with a notice to open it once or allow the site. If a redirect slips through, the tab goes back to where you were. Addresses you type, bookmarks and the back button are never blocked.
  5. Cosmetic filtering. A style sheet hides common ad containers so you don't see empty boxes.
  6. On-screen popups. Every few seconds, and whenever the page changes, it checks what sits on top of the middle and edges of the screen. A fixed layer covering the page, a dimmed backdrop, a cookie or newsletter bar, or an invisible click trap counts as a popup, unless it appeared right after you clicked something. It presses the popup's own close / "No thanks" / "Reject all" button, then Escape, then hides it, and gives scrolling back. A site that shows 3 such popups is remembered and handled automatically from then on. The same check runs inside embedded frames: on top of a video or an embedded player, invisible click layers, ad frames and link layers to other sites are removed, and the video and its controls are left. A layer whose click tried to open a popup that was blocked is removed right away, on any site.
  7. Memory. Every blocked popup, redirect and link is saved in the extension's local storage with how many times it happened. Your allow and block lists are saved there too.

"Same site" means the same registered domain: mail.google.com and www.google.com are one site; google.com and youtube.com are two.
